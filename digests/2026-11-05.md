## AWS What's New

**[Amazon Connect Customer lets agents pause and resume emails](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/connect-pause-resume-email/)**
Amazon Connect now lets customer service agents pause an email thread while waiting for approvals or information and resume it later. Paused time is tracked separately from active handling time, giving supervisors a more accurate view of how long agents spend working on emails.

**[Amazon WorkSpaces for AI agents now supports Linux and Windows 11 cloud desktops](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/workspaces-ai-agents-linux-windows-11)**
WorkSpaces for AI agents expands beyond Windows Server to support Linux and Windows 11 cloud desktops, letting each agent run on the OS its workflows require. Linux desktops include full root access, enabling agents to handle developer tasks such as building, testing, and automating agentic workflows in the same environments the workforce already uses.

**[Amazon Connect Customer prefills the email address for new threads started from cases](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/amazon-connect-cases-email-addresses/)**
Amazon Connect now automatically populates the customer's email address from their linked profile when agents open a new email thread from a case, eliminating manual lookup and reducing entry errors. The feature is available across nine AWS regions.

**[AWS Config now supports 10 new resource types](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/aws-config-new-resource-types)**
AWS Config added support for 10 additional resource types, including coverage for Amazon ElastiCache and Amazon QuickSight. Organizations with all-resource-type recording enabled will automatically track the new additions, which are also available in Config rules.

## AI Engineer

**[Don't Approve the AI Model. Approve the App - Doneyli De Jesus, Langfuse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-uBABM2AuFg)**
Langfuse's work with a top-five bank surfaces the governing principle for enterprise AI deployment: regulatory compliance targets the application, not the underlying model, requiring a certification workflow built around golden datasets, trace scoring, and hard quantitative gates.

- **Certify the app, not the model.** Banks need to approve a specific application configuration — on-premises, air-gapped, with defined behavior — which means the deployment gate sits on the app stack, not on model selection.
- **Golden datasets operationalize the gate.** Building a labeled reference set to score traces across multiple dimensions gives compliance teams a concrete, auditable pass/fail mechanism rather than a judgment call.
- **Tool augmentation can rescue a failing model.** A model scoring 97% on groundedness but only 60% on number accuracy failed the 85% threshold — adding a calculator tool pushed it over the gate.
- **Multi-dimensional scoring beats a single quality metric.** Scoring traces across several dimensions separately gives a more actionable picture than a combined score, isolating exactly which capability is below threshold.

**[Why Datasite Stopped Building Its Own Parsing - Adit Abraham, Reducto & Raj Bakhru, Datasite](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0PqGT2L6cZM)**
Datasite global CTO Raj Bakhru explains why a platform handling roughly 60% of deals over $100 million outsourced its document parsing rather than building in-house — a decision driven by the edge-case density of financial documents and a disciplined build-vs-buy framework applied across 250 engineers.

- **Financial document parsing is "all edge cases."** Parsing billions of pages of deal documents isn't a solved problem with a manageable tail — the exceptions are the dominant workload, making general-purpose parsers insufficient for high-stakes deal data.
- **Internal evals revealed the cost of mediocre parsing.** VDR Bench, Datasite's internal evaluation, showed that switching to better parsing raised recall by 22% — a direct argument for investing in parsing quality over shipping a working-but-imprecise in-house solution.
- **Build vs. buy is a strategic, not technical, decision.** With 250 engineers, Bakhru frames buy decisions around whether a capability is core differentiation or infrastructure a specialist can maintain at higher quality.
- **AI coding is reshaping hiring criteria.** The CTO notes that AI coding tools are changing which skills he prioritizes when hiring — a signal from a large engineering organization about how the talent bar is shifting.

**[Building the AI Agent Is Easy. Proving It's Safe Isn't - Jonathan Osborne, NatWest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5UgnatzpZ9E)**
NatWest's lead AI engineer recounts transitioning Cora — the bank's digital assistant handling roughly 100,000 conversations daily across 19 million customers — from scripted flows to AI agents, finding that safety validation is far harder than the initial build.

- **A routing funnel is the first line of safety.** Before anything reaches a model, NatWest built a funnel that limits which inputs ever touch the model, keeping the blast radius of model errors small by design.
- **Quality cannot be a single number.** Scoring quality across several independent dimensions rather than combining them into one metric is necessary; a single combined score obscures distinct failure modes.
- **High pass rates can hide severe gaps.** Human review showed an 83% pass rate — which sounds acceptable — but masked a 54-point spread between best and worst categories, a warning that summary statistics don't surface where the agent actually breaks.
- **Building the agent is the easy part.** The hardest work at NatWest was the evidence-gathering, scoring infrastructure, and human review processes needed to certify the agent was safe — not the agent itself.

**[Why AI Makes You 100x Faster but Not Your Company - Mitchell Troyanovsky, Basis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGtkTjn4120)**
Basis co-founder Mitchell Troyanovsky examines why individual engineers see massive AI productivity gains while company-wide productivity remains flat, arguing the gap closes only when agents are made opinionated, simple, and reliable rather than maximally flexible.

- **Opinionated agents spread; flexible ones stall.** Reliability requires constraining what an agent will do — the more choices an agent surfaces, the harder it is to verify and the less it gets adopted beyond early power users.
- **Behavior specs before outcome evals.** Troyanovsky argues outcome-level evaluations don't generalize to production; specifying desired behavior explicitly first creates a stable foundation for evaluation.
- **Outcome evals alone don't diagnose failures.** Two tools for understanding why an agent made a bad call: forking (replaying a decision branch) and sub-agent simulations (testing the specific sub-decision that went wrong).
- **The individual-to-company productivity gap is a deployment problem.** The 100x individual gain exists; the company-level gain doesn't appear until agents are simple and predictable enough to be trusted by non-builders across the organization.

**[Without Caching, Your AI Agent Costs 10x More - George Cameron, Artificial Analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=prGP0huECPo)**
Artificial Analysis co-founder George Cameron decomposes what actually drives AI agent costs in 2026 — input tokens, cache hit rate, and step count — and shows that caching is not a minor optimization but can produce a 10x cost difference on a single task.

- **Three levers, not one, drive agent cost.** Input tokens, cache hit rate, and number of steps each have significant independent impact; optimizing on model price alone misses most of the cost surface.
- **Caching is a 10x cost lever.** A single example showed an agent task costing roughly $21 with caching and over $200 without it — making cache architecture a first-class cost concern, not an afterthought.
- **Model selection should follow task requirements.** Cameron presents a framework for deciding when the most capable (and expensive) model is necessary vs. when a cheaper model produces the same result — essential as agent task costs compound across steps.
- **Cost variance across models is already extreme.** With tasks ranging from cents to dollars depending on model choice, teams running agents at scale need systematic cost benchmarking rather than ad-hoc model selection.
