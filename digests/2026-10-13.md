## The Knowledge Project Podcast

**[What Made Buffett Successful](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Um0JtnR0LQ)**
Morgan Housel, author of The Psychology of Money, argues on Shane Parrish's podcast that endurance — not genius, early decisions, or investment skill alone — is the dominant factor behind Warren Buffett's wealth. The evidence is stark: 99% of Buffett's net worth accumulated after his 60th birthday.

- **The compounding variable is time in the game.** Buffett's investment abilities were well-established decades before his largest gains arrived; what made them matter was an extraordinarily long, uninterrupted run.
- **The counterfactual test for survivorship.** Housel's framing question — would you have heard of Buffett if he had retired at 60? — surfaces how many people with comparable skill never reach the same result because they stopped.
- **Endurance as the underrated edge.** The implication is that most frameworks for "what made Buffett great" focus on the wrong variable; stopping the clock at 60 erases 99% of the outcome.

## Daniel Pink

**[Popular advice that is ruining your life](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tGnH1eo8wBE)**
Daniel Pink challenges widely accepted motivational advice — specifically "never give up" — by showing how it hardens into sunk-cost behavior that traps people in decisions they should exit. The problem is not the advice in the moment it is given; it is what it becomes once internalized as an obligation.

- **"Never give up" is the sunk-cost fallacy with a better marketing name.** The Arkes and Blumer theater study shows that full-price ticket buyers attended more performances than discounted buyers — not because the shows were better, but because prior spending exerted ongoing pressure on their choices.
- **Spent money can covertly govern present decisions.** The ticket price was irrelevant to the quality of any given evening; yet it still determined how people spent those evenings, demonstrating that sunk costs operate even when people know they are sunk.
- **The advice converts yesterday's spending into today's obligation.** Pink's argument is that "never give up" stops functioning as inspiration and starts functioning as a mechanism that makes exiting a losing position feel like a moral failure.

## AWS What's New

**[AWS Transfer Family supports custom ports for public SFTP servers](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/transfer-family-custom-ports-sftp/)**
AWS Transfer Family now allows public SFTP servers to accept connections on up to three configurable ports — port 22 or any port from 2000 to 65535 — rather than only port 22. Each port can also be assigned a different communication mode, accommodating clients that initiate SFTP connections in different ways on a single server.

**[Amazon ElastiCache for Valkey introduces ElastiCache Insights](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/amazon-elasticache-valkey-elasticache-insights/)**
ElastiCache Insights is a new pre-built CloudWatch dashboard for node-based Valkey clusters that consolidates cluster health, memory, compute, network, command throughput, latency, keyspace, and error metrics into a single view sourced from OpenTelemetry metrics published automatically by ElastiCache. It allows operators to monitor cache health across a region at a glance and detect when a node is approaching capacity limits.

**[Amazon OpenSearch Service now supports index-level encryption](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/opensearch-index-level-encryption)**
Amazon OpenSearch Service now supports encrypting individual indexes at rest with separate AWS KMS customer managed keys, allowing data with different ownership or sensitivity levels to be isolated under distinct keys on the same domain. This builds on the existing encryption-at-rest capability and is particularly useful for multi-tenant applications where each tenant's data can be protected by its own key.

## AI Engineer

**[Agents in Orbit: Workflows, Not Autopilot — Eduardo Simon & Javier Casal, Flywire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rAUyRDOFyHU)**
Flywire's Orbit platform treats AI model steps as declared components inside deterministic engineering workflows — governed by YAML definitions reviewed through the standard merge request process and deployed on existing infrastructure — rather than as autonomous agents with broad authority.

- **Read-only models produced 9,700 code review runs in 30 days.** The highest-volume workflow in the platform explicitly prevents the model from modifying code; read-only access was sufficient to generate significant value.
- **YAML as the governance layer.** Each workflow definition declares which tools and access levels each model step can use, and goes through merge request review alongside application code — giving the same audit trail and access control.
- **Predictable logic in code, judgment in the model.** Orbit's design principle is to keep deterministic control flow in code and use the model only where interpretation is needed.
- **No separate AI ops layer.** Running on the company's existing deployment infrastructure meant Orbit did not require a new platform to operate at scale.

**[Reliable, Smart Workflows for Finance, Wealth and Insurance — Karthic Rao, Perpetual](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8g0g1ncwzG0)**
Karthic Rao (Perpetual) argues that finance workflows demand a hybrid design: deterministic systems own control flow, evidence, and external actions, while models handle only the interpretation and judgment steps that fixed flowcharts cannot resolve — because a pure flowchart breaks on ambiguity and a pure improvising agent creates a different reliability problem.

- **Simple requests hide multi-day processes.** "Fill out a form for Mr. Harper" becomes a workflow that requires identifying the right household, surfacing missing information, contacting the client, obtaining a signature, and retaining evidence — too ambiguous for a fixed flowchart.
- **Two failure modes, not one.** A fixed flowchart breaks when it encounters ambiguity; an improvising agent fails by being unpredictably unreliable. The design at Perpetual refuses both failure modes.
- **Deterministic systems as the spine.** Control flow, evidence recording, and external actions stay in code; the model fills in the interpretation gaps rather than orchestrating the process.

**[Just-in-Time Ontologies: What Cheap Intelligence Means for Data — Hrishi Olickel, Southbridge AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fa3hQVIKShw)**
Hrishi Olickel (Southbridge AI) argues that data transformation cannot be solved with a universal schema or a confident agent — useful data structure must be derived just-in-time from the specific source, storage constraints, decision context, and assumptions the model brings to the task.

- **Multi-model consensus does not guarantee correctness.** A pipeline that stripped minus signs from loan amounts turned a net $784,000 into $5.3 million of debt, with multiple models all agreeing on the wrong result.
- **"Customer" is not a universal concept.** The same entity means something different to billing, support, and compliance; a single universal ontology cannot resolve that ambiguity.
- **Structure is a function of intent.** What constitutes useful data structure depends on what decision is being made, where the data will be stored, and what assumptions a model carries — not just what the raw data contains.

**[Your GPU Neighbor Can Hear Your Model — Branislav Brzak, Stealthium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SacJfmp33vM)**
Branislav Brzak (Stealthium) demonstrates an H100 timing side-channel attack that allows an adversarial co-tenant to infer model characteristics and workload categories from GPU scheduling patterns — without accessing the victim's memory or exploiting any software bug.

- **Three microseconds of timing signal is enough.** The attack probes only the attacker's own memory; the extra latency that reveals a neighboring tenant's activity is measured in microseconds.
- **No memory read, no exploit required.** The technique is purely observational — it identifies scheduling patterns via timing, not by reading victim data or leveraging a software vulnerability.
- **Workload fingerprinting, not prompt recovery.** The demonstrated capability is distinguishing workload categories (code, prose, JSON, retrieval) and inferring model depth and decoding speed; Brzak explicitly separates this from recovering actual prompts or answers.
- **Multi-tenant GPU slicing creates timing surfaces absent in dedicated instances.** The attack highlights a class of observability risk that is structural to shared GPU infrastructure.
