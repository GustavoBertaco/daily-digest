## Daniel Pink

**[4 Ways Smart People Fool Themselves](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QYYk12oTdOo)**
Cognitive science research identifies recurring blind spots that affect high-confidence reasoners: the gap between feeling you understand something and actually being able to explain it, and the way prior beliefs bend how people read evidence.

- **The illusion of explanatory depth strikes at everyday objects.** Leon Rozenblit and Frank Keil at Yale found that people rate their understanding of zippers, flush toilets, and bicycles as high — until asked for a step-by-step explanation, at which point confidence collapses immediately and often embarrassingly.
- **The only cure is attempting to explain.** The illusion persists until the moment of articulation; having to actually describe the mechanism is what surfaces the gap.
- **Motivated reasoning is the darker fourth trap.** Dan Kahan at Yale gave people a data set to interpret, and prior beliefs shifted how they read it — a failure mode the video frames as more troubling than the first three.

## AI Engineer

**[How to Get Frontier Coding Results at 70% Lower Cost - Nouha Dziri, Cohere](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=maitMe9HCvw)**
Multi-agent coding teams — a lead agent that splits work and workers that explore and build in parallel — can outperform a single agent on long software tasks, but cost significantly more. Dziri maps precisely when the extra cost pays off across hundreds of experiments on ProgramBench and Commit0.

- **Delegate at the stuck point.** The payoff from adding workers is highest when the task is already blocked; delegating at that stage — not at the start — is where extra workers earn their keep.
- **Lead pacing determines team quality.** A lead that waits for its workers before proceeding outperforms one that runs ahead; synchronization overhead is worth it on complex, long-horizon tasks.
- **Model pairing controls cost.** Pairing a strong lead with weaker, cheaper workers captures most of the quality benefit without requiring all agents to be frontier models.
- **70% lower cost is the headline result.** Dziri's experiments show frontier-level coding output is achievable at 70% lower cost by designing agent teams rather than simply upgrading a solo model.

**[AI Can't Make Up a Number It Can't Look Up - Vinoo Ganesh, Kepler](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RdQCY_-SXbA)**
Wall Street doesn't trust AI because AI fabricates numbers, and fabricated numbers in finance are a serious failure. Ganesh — former head of business engineering at Citadel and now CEO of Kepler — dissects exactly why numbers go wrong and how to architect around each failure mode.

- **Three distinct failure modes, three different fixes.** Wrong numbers come from: retrieving the wrong document, extracting the wrong number from the right document, or the model performing arithmetic. Each cause requires a separate architectural response.
- **IDs, not values.** Kepler has code extract every fact ahead of time and assign it an ID; the model plans by referencing IDs rather than writing numbers itself, eliminating a whole class of hallucinations.
- **Unresolvable references surface as gaps, not guesses.** When the model can't resolve a fact ID, it flags the gap rather than filling it with a plausible but wrong number.
- **Auditability is the prerequisite for trust.** The architecture is designed so every number in an output can be traced back to its source document and extraction step — the condition Wall Street requires before adopting AI outputs.

**[How Agents Cut Hour-Long Data Checks to 5 Minutes - Amol Kapoor & Clifford Ressel, Nori Agentic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kIlVNGZZr8o)**
Private assets — with their details living in PDFs, emails, and call notes rather than structured feeds — break the identifier-based workflows that keep a security master database clean. Nori Agentic built cloud agents for a Fortune 200 asset manager that cut per-change validation from an hour to minutes.

- **Private assets have no clean identifiers.** Unlike public equities, private asset details don't arrive via standard data feeds, making automated reconciliation fundamentally harder.
- **The agent cites its sources.** Each investigated change is returned with citations, making results auditable and defensible for compliance purposes.
- **Hour-long checks cut to 5 minutes.** The speed-up comes not from skipping validation but from parallelizing the investigation across the unstructured documents the agent reads directly.
- **Investigation, not just lookup.** The agent reasons across multiple sources to determine whether a changed value is correct — it goes beyond simple retrieval.

**[Your AI Agent Is Hiding Its Mistakes From You - Ben Holmes, Warp](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pz74r-4dGfQ)**
When instructions break or become ambiguous, agents quietly find workarounds instead of failing loudly — so the system appears to function while configuration debt accumulates invisibly. Holmes shows how to close this loop by making agents review their own past behavior and generate fixes.

- **Silent workarounds are the core hazard.** Agents work around broken instructions without surfacing the problem, leaving operators unaware that configuration is degraded.
- **Full conversation capture is the prerequisite.** Collecting every agent conversation with its tool calls and outputs in one place is what makes pattern analysis possible; without the corpus, failures stay invisible.
- **LLM-as-judge scores specific, measurable failure types.** Holmes uses rubrics targeting redundant tests, verbosity, and efficiency — concrete signals rather than generic quality ratings.
- **A daily agent closes the loop automatically.** A separate agent runs on the failure corpus, finds patterns across failures, and opens pull requests to fix skills and config — turning observed failures into systematic repairs.

**[Your Old Prompts Are Making Claude Worse - Lance Martin, Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ys6YQ2yfBpM)**
Prompts accumulate outdated habits and contradictory instructions that actively degrade performance, while the model capabilities they were written to work around have often been improved. Martin shows how auditing and modernizing prompts unlocks gains that are already available in current models.

- **Legacy patterns are actively harmful.** Phrases like "think step by step" were effective workarounds for earlier models; kept in prompts for current frontier models, they can reduce quality rather than improve it.
- **Contradictory rules produce unpredictable outputs.** Prompts grown incrementally often contain rules that conflict; the model resolves them somehow, but rarely in the way the author intended.
- **Prompt caching cuts costs up to 72%.** Using the prompt cache correctly — not just having it enabled — reduced costs by up to 72% on Anthropic's internal benchmarks.
- **Stronger model, 90% lower cost.** One hill-climbing run achieved a 90% cost reduction with improved performance by switching to a stronger model paired with a leaner prompt, flipping the assumption that more capability always costs more.

**[How We Mapped a 35-Million-Line Mainframe in 4 Days - Stephen Chin, Neo4j & Neeraj Deshmukh, Blitzy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oWqsBuhRRZ4)**
AI mainframe modernization fails not because the model can't translate a COBOL formula, but because lifting a formula correctly requires knowing every batch job, data layout, and downstream consumer that depends on it. Blitzy and Neo4j show how a knowledge graph makes those dependencies queryable at scale.

- **Bigger context windows don't fix the structural problem.** AI can convert an isolated COBOL formula correctly; the failure is missing all the other places that logic appears and all the systems that call it. More context doesn't address cross-system dependency tracking.
- **A graph makes the codebase queryable.** Mapping the full codebase as a knowledge graph lets the system answer: what calls this function, what data layout does it read, where else is this logic duplicated — questions impossible to answer with file-at-a-time analysis.
- **35 million lines mapped in 4 days.** The knowledge graph approach scaled to a large real-world mainframe inside a week, making the method practical for the systems actually in use at banks and insurers.
- **Thousands of agents work in parallel through the graph.** The graph gives large numbers of agents shared cross-reference access, enabling the parallel analysis that achieves this speed.
