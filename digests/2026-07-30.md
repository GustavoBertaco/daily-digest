## AI Engineer

**[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Factory — Eno Reyes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wpOA-UXynoM)**
Factory's forward deployed engineers are embedded with the largest customers and treat the relationship as a closed-loop production system: real-world signals flow continuously back into how Droid (Factory's AI agent) gets built, making field work and product improvement the same motion.

- **FDE as a signal pipeline, not a services role.** Engineers embedded with customers exist primarily to funnel real-world signal back into the product — the customer outcome and the product improvement are the same motion, not parallel workstreams.
- **The software factory metaphor structures the whole job.** Eno Reyes frames the work as: signals in → plans → validation stages → shipped outcomes. Most organizations run this loop badly because they don't treat it as an engineering system requiring deliberate investment.
- **Model independence is non-negotiable.** The harness built around the agent matters more than which underlying model runs; building tightly to a single model is a brittleness trap the team explicitly designs around.

**[AI tools for Forward Deployed Engineering — Vasuman Moza, Varick Agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic)**
Varick Agents' entire product premise is that enterprises won't replace entrenched systems — so instead of selling migration, forward deployed engineers map existing processes, including undocumented handoffs, and layer agents on top of what's already running.

- **The migration constraint is the product brief.** When a customer has sunk five million dollars and five years into SAP, "just migrate" is not an option. The FDE's design space starts from that constraint, not the ideal architecture.
- **Process archaeology before automation.** Vasuman Moza describes mapping purchase-order-to-invoice reconciliations and undocumented handoffs between departments before automating anything — understanding the informal system is the prerequisite.
- **Drop agents on top, not inside.** The deployment model wraps existing systems rather than replacing them, letting the enterprise retain its current stack while gaining automation coverage across both documented and undocumented processes.

**[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Cognition — Jia Wu](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns)**
Cognition's deployed engineering team measures against customer-visible outcomes rather than token efficiency, claiming approximately an 82% reduction in targeted work — a figure grounded in a baseline-then-delta methodology that establishes numbers before deployment and again after full activation.

- **Measure before you land.** Jia Wu insists on establishing a customer workload baseline before Devin is activated; post-deployment gains only carry weight if there is a pre-deployment number to compare against.
- **Outcome metrics, not token metrics.** While most of the coding agent market quietly optimizes for token usage, Cognition tracks what the customer can actually see — completed work, not compute efficiency.
- **Adoption is non-linear by design.** One team using the agent is described as a step function; value multiplies as more of the organization activates, making ramp-up sequencing a deliberate strategic choice rather than an organic rollout.
- **~82% reduction on targeted work.** The headline figure is grounded in the baseline-delta approach above, designed to distinguish it from vanity benchmarks common in the coding agent space.

**[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Ramp — Leo Mehr](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A)**
Ramp's FDE philosophy is built around a counter-intuitive discipline: resisting the reflexive yes. Leo Mehr treats constant scoping — weighing what actually matters against the full queue — as the primary job, not a precursor to execution.

- **"Always be scoping" as a first principle.** The FDE role is a judgment function first: deciding what matters against the full queue is the work, and execution follows from that decision rather than driving it.
- **Reflexive yes harms teams and customers alike.** The Friday-night SAP S4 HANA integration example illustrates how unconstrained commitment displaces higher-value work and ultimately disserves the customer who made the request.
- **Palantir lineage, adapted to fintech enterprise.** Ramp's FDE function is rooted in Palantir's field-deployment tradition but is pointed at enterprise customers in a fintech context, showing how the model adapts across product categories without losing its core discipline.

**[The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering — Natalie Meurer, Sierra](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE)**
The dirty secret is definitional: "forward deployed engineering" has never described a consistent role. At Palantir it began as literal on-site firefighting, then absorbed DevOps, data integration, ontology work in Slate and Foundry, and solution architecture — leaving the title meaning too many different things to mean much at all.

- **The role started as literal geography.** The original Palantir FDE was physically co-located at the customer site; the first job was simply keeping the platform operational — a support and firefighting function before it became anything more.
- **Scope inflation eroded the label.** Over time the role absorbed DevOps, data integration, ontology work, and solution architecture, accumulating responsibilities without ever narrowing the definition of what forward deployed actually means.
- **The title has spread faster than its meaning.** Natalie Meurer's point is that every company now hiring FDEs is filling a different role under the same name, creating genuine confusion for candidates and hiring managers across the industry.

**[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Decagon — Sunny Rekhi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0)**
At Decagon, forward deployed engineering divides into two distinct tracks — configuring the agent brain (instructions and handoff rules per enterprise) and proactive product engineering (anticipating enterprise needs before they are requested) — with the boundary between those tracks and the core product team intentionally blurred.

- **Two jobs under one title.** The FDE role splits into agent configuration — what the customer support agent says, when it escalates to a human — and forward-looking product engineering that anticipates what each new enterprise will need next.
- **Handoff logic is a first-class deliverable.** The rules determining when the AI hands a conversation to a human are configured per enterprise by FDEs, not baked in generically, making them a meaningful product artifact rather than an afterthought.
- **The FDE/product boundary is intentionally porous.** Sunny Rekhi observes that the line between forward deployed and product engineering is blurred by design at Decagon, so field feedback flows directly into the roadmap without passing through a translation layer.

**[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Kepler — Vinoo Ganesh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OMHGsUZiqA)**
Vinoo Ganesh's core argument — drawn from seven years at Palantir, including running its SWE-to-FDE rotation program — is that forward deployed engineering is product strategy in disguise: the field engineer's job is to cut through what customers ask for and ship what they actually need first, then build the rest from the trust that creates.

- **Ship the Monday morning need, not the full spec.** A shipping and dispatch customer wanted alerts, a massive BI tool, and a dev environment; what they needed first was one Slack message. Ganesh's principle: ship that immediately, earn the trust, then build the full thing.
- **FDE is product strategy, not a sales motion.** Seven years at Palantir — including running the SWE-to-FDE rotation program — shaped a view that field work is where product decisions get validated in the real world, not where sales commitments get executed.
- **The rotation model makes FDE skills transferable.** Palantir's program deliberately moved software engineers through forward deployed rotations, implying the perspective is learnable and the skill transfer runs bidirectionally between field and core product.

**[Forward Deployed Engineering 101 — Kevin Bai, Anthropic, ex Palantir & Rippling Founding FDE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KwhgfwOSToQ)**
Kevin Bai's primer starts from deal economics: forward deployed engineering exists in the gap between what most software companies sell and the half-million-dollar-plus average contract value tier — where the buyer is non-technical and the product cannot be self-configured without embedded engineering help.

- **Deal economics define where FDE is needed.** Very few public software companies crack half a million dollars in average contract value; FDE is the mechanism that makes that tier viable when buyers can't evaluate or configure the product themselves.
- **The loan model reframes the customer relationship.** The customer isn't buying a product or hiring a consultant — they are borrowing engineers who build a real solution on top of the vendor's platform. Kevin Bai frames this structure as fundamental to what FDE actually is.
- **Non-technical buyers require a different delivery model.** When the buyer can't evaluate or configure the product themselves, shipping software alone is not a complete delivery; the FDE closes the gap between what was sold and what the customer can actually use.

**[Serving 2 Million Models Without Melting: Scaling the Hugging Face Hub — Arek Borucki, Hugging Face](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lyL5QhgIOxc)**
At 3 million public models, 14 million users, and a million datasets, search on the Hugging Face Hub can no longer rely on naive ranking — Arek Borucki describes how the Hub separates metadata from artifacts and replaced regex-based ranking with a dedicated relevance layer to keep query latency acceptable at that scale.

- **Metadata and artifacts live in different stores.** MongoDB Atlas holds model metadata; S3 holds the artifacts. This decoupling lets search and compute scale independently without bloating the query path with artifact data.
- **Full-text search on Apache Lucene, surfaced through MongoDB Atlas.** The stack provides proven text search capabilities while keeping operational complexity manageable across a catalog of 3 million models and growing.
- **Regex ranking broke at scale.** As the catalog grew, the relevance layer had to move beyond regex matching — naive ranking degrades before query performance does when the corpus reaches millions of entries.
- **14 million users and a million datasets compound the pressure.** Datasets add a second dimension to scale alongside models, meaning search must handle a multi-type catalog at the same latency target within a single query path.
