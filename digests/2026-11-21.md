## AI Engineer

**[The Sweet Side of the Bitter Lesson — Thariq Shihipar, Independent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xRJMiaUoD_8)**
At CODE Summit 2026, Thariq Shihipar revisits the Bitter Lesson — the finding that general methods leveraging compute tend to outperform hand-crafted approaches — and argues there is a "sweet side": AI's smooth interior creates space for practitioners to design workflows that preserve creative control rather than cede it entirely to the model.

- **Creative control is the core risk.** Shihipar's entry point is personal: vibe-coding a fighting game left him unable to explain how his own characters behaved, illustrating how agent-assisted development can erode author understanding even as it accelerates output.
- **Jagged frontier vs. smooth interior.** The framing contrasts AI's inconsistent capability at the edges (jagged) with its reliable performance on routine tasks (smooth); which side of that boundary you are working on determines your design choices.
- **Readable specifications as a discipline.** His live demo favors explicit, human-readable specs over opaque generation — keeping the developer in the loop and making agent behavior auditable and correctable rather than a black box.
- **Adapt the toolchain rather than fight the model.** Rather than resisting AI tendencies, he shapes the stack — Bash, React, MCP, custom scripting languages, and IDE workflows — to channel agent behavior toward predictable, interpretable outputs.
- **Software renaissance as the upside.** The argument concludes that coding agents done right amplify craft rather than replace it, but only if practitioners design for transparency and legibility from the outset.
