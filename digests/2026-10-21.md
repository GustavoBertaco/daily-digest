## AWS What's New

**[Amazon Connect Customer Global Resiliency now provides business users visibility into resiliency readiness across linked Regions](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/amazon-connect-global-resiliency-readiness-overview)**
Amazon Connect Global Resiliency now includes a dedicated Global Resiliency page in the admin website, giving administrators a single consolidated view of Region switch readiness. The page surfaces overall readiness status and tracks which natively replicated resources — phone numbers, users, and others — are in sync, pending synchronization, or need action.

**[Amazon CloudWatch Omni is now available in additional AWS Regions](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/amazon-cloudwatch-omni-8-regions/)**
CloudWatch Omni, AWS's AI-powered observability experience, is now available in eight additional AWS Regions. It combines OpenTelemetry interoperability with CloudWatch's scale, accessible as a standalone web experience with SSO or as a local IDE extension.

**[EC2 Image Builder now integrates with ASG for faster image builds](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/ec2-image-builder-asg-faster-builds/)**
EC2 Image Builder can now reuse already-running instances from an existing Auto Scaling Group instead of launching and bootstrapping new instances for each build. Specifying an ASG in the infrastructure configuration lets Image Builder apply build components directly to a running instance, cutting the time overhead of cold starts.

**[Grok 4.7 is now available on Amazon Bedrock in AWS GovCloud (US)](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/amazon-bedrock-grok-4-7-govcloud/)**
SpaceXAI's Grok 4.7 is now accessible on Amazon Bedrock in AWS GovCloud (US), extending a frontier model built for coding, agentic tasks, and knowledge work into the regulated government cloud. Grok 4.7 improves on Grok 4.6 with better mixed-document handling, more reliable repo-scale coding with planning and error recovery, and enhanced browser-use agents for form filling and portal navigation.

## AI Engineer

**[Shipping AI Into 5 Major Banks Without a 12-Month Compliance Wait — Al Chen, Cisco](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bz1HFxQA1o8)**
Al Chen describes what it actually took to get AI deployed inside five major banks without a year-long compliance bottleneck — the security review process is the real product constraint, not model capability, and the teams who internalize that ship faster.

- **Security review IS the product requirement.** What banks check before allowing software in is the design spec. Teams that treat compliance as a gate to clear after building lose months; teams that build to the checklist from day one do not.
- **CVE remediation with agents writing and testing exploits.** Rather than patching vulnerabilities and hoping, the pipeline used agents to write exploits proving the CVE, then verify the fix — producing evidence the bank's security team could actually evaluate.
- **Backup and restore verification is non-negotiable.** Banks require proof of data recovery capability; this had to be a first-class, demonstrable step in the build pipeline.
- **PII leaks through LLM judges.** Using an LLM as a judge for evaluation creates a new vector for PII exfiltration — one that doesn't exist in traditional software and requires explicit mitigations in regulated deployments.
- **Zero leakage and data sovereignty are never free.** These properties require ongoing operational cost and architectural choices; they cannot be declared and assumed.

**[The Investor's Edge Lives in Their Head — Anekha Sokhal, Moshi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UHbODjuviAU)**
Anekha Sokhal, founder and CEO of Moshi, argues that investment alpha increasingly resides in tacit knowledge inside the investor's head rather than in any structured dataset — and that AI should absorb the quantitative grind while the human retains decision authority.

- **The edge isn't in the database.** Any data set you can buy, your competitors can also buy. The differentiated context is the pattern recognition investors develop over years of seeing deals, markets, and outcomes — and that doesn't live in a schema.
- **AI as a one-person quant team.** Moshi's design gives a single investor the analytical throughput of a full quant team, compressing research cycles without replacing judgment on the final call.
- **One-person pods as the emerging fund structure.** AI makes it viable for a single investor to operate at a scale that previously required a much larger organization.
- **Split the grind from the decision.** The practical architecture: AI handles data gathering, screening, and quantitative modeling; the human holds conviction and makes the bet.

**[Why AI Agents Will Need Their Own Bank Accounts — Lincoln Murr, Coinbase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_HwzoYrZ7s)**
Lincoln Murr traces Coinbase's path from AgentKit — giving an AI a wallet as an early experiment — to x402, an open standard built on HTTP's long-dormant 402 "payment required" code, now handling hundreds of millions of agent transactions per month.

- **x402 activates a 30-year-old reserved status code.** HTTP 402 was defined in 1996 as "Payment Required" and left unused. Coinbase's x402 protocol turns it into a live machine-to-machine payment standard for agentic transactions.
- **Scale has already arrived.** x402 handling hundreds of millions of agent transactions monthly signals this is no longer a research prototype — the infrastructure for agentic finance is in production.
- **Two distinct categories of agentic finance.** Agents that spend money (paying for compute, APIs, data) and agents that grow money (running trades on instructions) are separate product categories with different risk, compliance, and trust requirements.
- **Microtransactions as a structural alternative to advertising.** If agents pay per use at the point of consumption, the ad-funded model that dominates the consumer web loses its position as the default monetization layer.

**[How to Know When an AI Agent Can Work Unsupervised — Evan Kormos, Coinbase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_4Ph2dhmUFI)**
Evan Kormos presents a DAG/loop process model for regulated financial workflows, where evals and execution traces function as a confidence mechanism that lets agents graduate from full human supervision to exception-only review.

- **Policy is the original system prompt.** Regulated financial workflows already encode constraints with the precision that agent instructions require — making finance a stronger structural fit for agentic deployment than less-formalized domains.
- **Loops are the primary risk surface.** A DAG terminates; a loop can compound errors indefinitely. The trustworthiness of a looping agent is exactly as strong as the reliability of its exit condition check.
- **Evals and traces replace binary supervision.** Rather than switching from supervised to unsupervised, evals on live agent runs produce a continuous confidence signal that drives a dynamic supervision ratio.
- **Three-stage maturity model.** Fully supervised → sampled supervision → humans handling exceptions only. Each stage transition is gated on demonstrated eval reliability at the prior stage, not on calendar time or team confidence.

**[Smarter Models Killed the Harness. Here's What's Left — Jediah Katz, SpaceXAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Y4sbZgBdnc)**
Jediah Katz argues that the data flywheel has made sophisticated coding harnesses redundant for pure intelligence — models now learn what harnesses used to patch in — but that the harness matters more than ever for the things models structurally cannot absorb.

- **The data flywheel absorbed harness intelligence.** Skills harnesses once injected — file navigation, code search, context assembly, error recovery — are now trained directly into models at scale. Adding a smarter harness no longer adds coding capability.
- **The harness opens new capability frontiers.** Grokbot is cited as an example of what becomes possible through the harness that the model alone cannot reach — new surfaces and interactions, not incremental intelligence.
- **Infrastructure, trust, and security remain harness-owned.** Orchestration reliability, sandboxed execution, security guarantees, and efficiency optimizations cannot be learned from a training corpus — they are the harness's irreplaceable domain.
- **90% of agent work moves to simpler execution paths.** The prediction: the vast majority of routine agentic coding tasks will migrate to cheaper, lower-overhead pipelines as models commoditize the intelligence layer.
