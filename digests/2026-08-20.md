## AI Engineer

**[The Next Medium: Why Real-Time Interactive Video Changes Everything — Ahmed Ahres, Reactor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5dCAmSDOAjI)**
Ahmed Ahres argues that real-time interactive video is a change of medium rather than a speed improvement — drawing on the GPS analogy: before GPS you consulted a map someone else made, but afterwards your own position became something you could act on continuously. Generated video today sits on the wrong side of that inflection.

- **Medium shift, not speedup.** The GPS case is exact: the technology didn't make maps faster, it created a new kind of spatial awareness that enabled a new category of application. Ahres applies the same logic to real-time video generation.
- **The viewfinder enabled TikTok.** Once film cameras showed what you were shooting as you shot it, you could adjust in the moment — that feedback loop is what ultimately made short-form social video possible. Ahres positions real-time video generation as an equivalent enabling layer.
- **Interactivity is the medium, latency is the gating factor.** The argument is that until you can act on the generated output in real time, you are still in the "consult a map" era — useful, but categorically different from what becomes possible once the loop closes.

**[Infra behind Krea 2: How to train and serve at scale — Gabriel Jorge Menezes, Krea.ai](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=byn9PURoBNY)**
Gabriel Jorge Menezes argues that training a generative video model at scale requires instrumentation that standard tooling does not provide — GPU utilization is actively misleading, and the metrics that actually matter have to be built custom.

- **GPU utilization is a lie.** It read 100% throughout pretraining while the cluster was nowhere near well used. Tensor core utilization is the meaningful signal, and Menezes watched it climb as training resolution stepped from 128 pixels up to 1024.
- **InfiniBand health is invisible by default.** No off-the-shelf tooling exports InfiniBand counters; most cluster failures turned out to be cross-node communication issues, so they built their own collection infrastructure.
- **Resolution curriculum drives actual cluster efficiency.** Stepping training resolution upward progressively — rather than training at full resolution from the start — improved tensor core utilization in measurable increments.

**[Generative Video at the Speed of Light — Keegan McCallum, uRun](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xln-On3syJk)**
Keegan McCallum argues that the interesting axis in generative video has moved past quality — cost and real-time interactivity are now the frontier, and the economics are already comparable to what developers spend on coding tokens in a single session.

- **The cost benchmark is concrete.** Ten dollars buys roughly three hours of continuously generated video; fifty dollars buys fifteen. Many developers in the room admitted spending that much on coding tokens within a single hour — the relative scale puts video generation on a plausible cost curve.
- **Real-time vs. high-quality is a genuine engineering tradeoff, not a progress question.** A real-time generation placed next to a minutes-long render shows the slower clip with better motion — but at roughly 100x the cost. The choice is explicit, not a matter of one approach being immature.
- **Quality crossed the threshold; speed is now the differentiator.** The competitive and product design question is no longer whether outputs are good enough but whether they are fast enough to enable a new interaction model.

**[Voice agents with Realtime Video — Sidney Primas, LemonSlice](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z1dqv74SpUs)**
Sidney Primas explains that sustaining a real-time video avatar for hours without reset requires solving a compounding error problem: every generated block inherits errors from all prior blocks, and the errors accumulate.

- **Real-time avatars can only look backward.** Future frames don't exist yet when a block is generated, so every output inherits the accumulated errors of everything before it. Duration is the enemy, not just frame quality.
- **Training with a causal attention mask enforces the real constraint.** LemonSlice trains the model with an attention mask that replicates the forward-only temporal structure at inference time, so the model learns how to handle compounding historical context.
- **Duration is the engineering target.** A Teddy Roosevelt avatar running in a replica Oval Office has sustained eight hours without reset; a second deployment is being built for sixteen. The specific hour targets are the benchmark for whether the error-compounding problem is solved.

**[While my guitar gently speaks — Todd Fisher, Philo Ventures](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_Txocq-Lrw)**
Todd Fisher's guitar that answers audience questions — routing speech recognition through a local model and outputting through the strings — places local AI inference at the end of a hardware augmentation lineage running from the electric pickup through the talk box.

- **The loop is complete and real-time.** A microphone routes through speech recognition into a local model, and the reply is pushed back out through the guitar strings. Someone asked the guitar what reality is; the guitar answered.
- **The lineage matters for the engineering framing.** Fisher traces electric guitar augmentation from the pickup and amplifier, through stomp boxes, to Peter Frampton sending guitar sound down a physical hose into his mouth — each step made the instrument interact with something external. The local model is the latest link.
- **"How hard could it be" is a legitimate research method.** The project began as a simple question, not a product brief. The answer — harder than expected, but tractable — is the whole story.

**[The Next Game Engine Won't Have a Manual — Arturo Nunez, Nereu](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VBCDhRrvlYo)**
Arturo Nunez diagnoses the core problem with coding agents in game development: their context sits on the game engine's vocabulary rather than the game's, causing them to reinvent every primitive from scratch, slightly differently, on every call.

- **The reinvention problem is structural, not a model capability gap.** Ask a coding agent for a follow-camera and it produces a new one from scratch every time, because the context it receives describes a mesh, renderer, animator, rigid body, collider, and audio source — not a "character that moves."
- **Almost all provided context is boilerplate.** Nearly everything required to describe a character in a conventional engine is already present in every character in every game. The actual movement logic is a small fraction of what the agent has to read and regenerate.
- **The fix is a semantic layer, not a better model.** Nereu's thesis is that a game engine designed for agent interaction would expose game-native concepts directly, eliminating the vocabulary mismatch and the redundant reinvention it causes.

**[Building an Agentic Video Editor for Mass Consumer — Ekaterina Deyneka, Reelful](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pPj_tjlvYjA)**
Ekaterina Deyneka's Reelful is built for the gap between recording and posting: nearly every conference attendee had recorded video; almost none had posted any of it. The agent takes raw footage plus a line of direction and handles the entire editing and production pipeline.

- **The gap is the market.** Recording is easy; post-production is where footage dies. The observation that almost no hands stayed up for "actually posted it" is the entire product thesis.
- **The agent handles full post-production.** Drop in raw footage with a line of direction and the agent finds usable moments, cuts them together, and generates captions, music, voiceover, and B-roll. The user provides intent; the agent provides craft.
- **The engineering frame is agentic pipeline design, not video tooling.** Deyneka's framing positions Reelful as an agentic workflow problem for an AI engineering audience — the video domain is the application layer, not the primary technical challenge.

**[Training Krea 2: What matters in generative model training — Sangwu Lee, Krea.ai](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-tviRdpmHvs)**
Sangwu Lee argues that large production image models optimize for consistency at the cost of stylistic range — Krea 2 deliberately trades in the other direction, prioritizing fast generation and variety over predictable, polished outputs.

- **Consistency is a trap for creative exploration.** The most reliable way to render a person in a big model is to produce the most boring average version in the center of the frame. Ask for a burning skull and every output returns clean and nearly identical. This is a feature for production use cases and a constraint for creative iteration.
- **Krea 2 is optimized for studios that don't yet know what they want.** By trading consistency for fast generation and stylistic range, the model positions itself for early-stage creative exploration rather than high-confidence production rendering.
- **The medium variant is now open source.** This is the concrete, actionable artifact from the talk: the Krea 2 medium variant is publicly available, making the range-and-speed tradeoff accessible beyond Krea's internal work.
