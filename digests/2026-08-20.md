## AWS What's New

**[Launching External Web Access for Web Search on Amazon Bedrock](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/amazon-bedrock-web-access-web-search/)**
Amazon Bedrock's Web Search feature now supports an `external_web_access` parameter that lets models retrieve content directly from the public web, extending the earlier zero-egress, server-side implementation. Enabling external access requires granting the bedrock-web-search role the appropriate permission.

**[Web Search in Amazon Bedrock AgentCore adds domain and published date filtering, expands to Europe and Asia Pacific](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/web-search-amazon-bedrock/)**
Web Search in Amazon Bedrock AgentCore now supports per-request domain filtering and published-date filtering, giving agents runtime control over which sources and time windows are searched. The feature also expands to Europe and Asia Pacific regions.

**[Amazon CloudWatch log Centralization now supports log group tag propagation](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/amazon-cloudwatch-centralization-tag-propogation/)**
Amazon CloudWatch Centralization now propagates log group tags from source accounts to destination log groups, ensuring cost, ownership, and compliance tags travel with centralized data. CloudWatch Centralization aggregates logs from multiple accounts and regions into a single destination account.

**[Amazon SageMaker notebooks now support trusted identity propagation](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/amazon-sagemaker/)**
Amazon SageMaker Notebooks now support Trusted Identity Propagation (TIP) with Athena, Redshift, and EMR Serverless, routing each notebook user's IAM Identity Center identity through to Lake Formation for row- and column-level access control. This eliminates the need to share a single broad execution role across users.

**[Amazon CloudWatch pipelines adds GeoIP, RDS, and XML processors](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/cloudwatch-geoip-rds-xml/)**
Amazon CloudWatch pipelines adds three new processors at ingestion time: an RDS log parser, an XML parser, and a GeoIP enrichment processor, making log data immediately queryable without separate reprocessing. CloudWatch pipelines is fully managed and routes telemetry to CloudWatch without infrastructure management.

**[AWS Cost Anomaly Detection supports third-party models on Amazon Bedrock](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/aws-cost-anomaly-detection-bedrock-3P/)**
AWS Cost Anomaly Detection now covers spend on third-party foundation models on Amazon Bedrock — including Anthropic Claude and other provider-hosted models — using machine learning to flag unusual usage automatically. Teams running production generative AI workloads get anomaly detection on Bedrock model spend alongside the rest of their AWS costs.

**[Amazon OpenSearch Ingestion is now available in GovCloud Regions](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/opensearch-ingestion-available-govcloud-regions)**
Amazon OpenSearch Ingestion is now available in AWS GovCloud (US-East) and AWS GovCloud (US-West), extending the fully managed ingestion tier to government cloud environments. The service supports ingestion into both OpenSearch managed clusters and serverless collections.

**[AWS Marketplace launches support for Amazon Lightsail](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/aws-marketplace-launch-ami-amazon-lightsail)**
AWS Marketplace now supports deploying select AMIs directly on Amazon Lightsail, combining Marketplace software with Lightsail's fixed-price bundles that include compute, storage, and data transfer. Deployment takes a few clicks.

**[AWS announces a new Availability Zone in the Europe (London) Region](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/aws-new-availability-zone-europe/)**
AWS has added a fourth Availability Zone to the Europe (London) Region (eu-west-2), expanding capacity for AI and ML workloads including EC2 Trn3 and P6 accelerated instances. The addition also increases fault isolation options for customers building highly available architectures in the region.

**[AWS Lambda MicroVMs is now available in 5 additional AWS regions](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/lambda-microvms-5-additional-regions)**
AWS Lambda MicroVMs is now available in five additional regions — Mumbai, Singapore, Sydney, Frankfurt, and Stockholm — bringing the total to 10 supported regions. Lambda MicroVMs provides VM-level isolation, near-instant launch and resume, and state preservation for executing user or AI-generated code.

# 🤖 AI & Machine Learning

## AI Engineer

**[Why Your Enterprise Tech Stack Isn't Ready for AI Agents — Christopher Lovejoy & Saul Howard](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mav15aW9lLM)**
Christopher Lovejoy and Saul Howard describe a recurring failure mode: AI agent proof of concepts that meet all technical targets stall at compliance review because audit trails required by enterprise frameworks are fundamentally different from developer logs.

- **A passing PoC and a production-ready agent are not the same thing.** The metrics that make a room happy — accuracy, speed, cost — are not the metrics compliance evaluates. The gap appears the first time an audit question is asked.
- **An audit trail is a legal artifact, not a log file.** Under the frameworks enterprises actually operate under, it must record every action an agent took, every data surface it touched, and the authorization for each — a requirement that typical agent observability does not satisfy.
- **This is a structural readiness gap, not a model capability question.** Lovejoy and Howard's point is that the enterprise tech stack itself — not the agent — is what is not ready. Most enterprise infrastructure was not built to capture agent-level provenance at the required fidelity.

**[Don't be data poor — Anuj Iravane, Anterior](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XAsb7MIAzm8)**
Anuj Iravane explains how Anterior builds healthcare AI evals when the most valuable training signal — multi-hundred-page fax bundles containing a patient's full clinical trajectory — is contractually prohibited from being retained, derived from, or even anonymized.

- **Roughly 70% of medical communication still moves by fax.** The raw input is scanned bundles running past 300 pages per patient, containing handwriting, checkboxes, tables, and images — what Iravane describes as an observation through a fuzzy lens over a lifespan.
- **The best eval data is the data you cannot keep.** Contracts rule out retaining the data, deriving from it, or holding redacted or anonymized copies. The signal disappears the moment the inference completes.
- **"Data poor" names a specific engineering constraint.** The talk is about how to build rigorous evals when you cannot accumulate the data that would make those evals reliable — a problem specific to privacy-constrained healthcare AI.

**[How to build an AI-Native Health Company — Dan Feng, Maven Clinic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WJRdLNhrsLQ)**
Dan Feng argues that the cost of software development at Maven Clinic has moved from implementation to argumentation — building takes minutes now, so extended requirement-settling is the expensive step, and planning horizons changed to match.

- **The cost structure of building flipped.** Implementation used to justify weeks of upfront requirement work. Now building takes minutes, and spending time arguing about requirements is what is expensive.
- **Planning horizons at Maven Clinic are two to four weeks.** A one-year view survives only as directional intent, on the assumption models will handle needs that can't yet be specified. Real commitments run two to four weeks out.
- **Requirements became working documents, not gates.** Long requirement documents gave way to a page or two — designed to be argued with, not approved before work begins.

**[Trading Desks to Clinical Trials: Parallels in Applied Vertical AI — Ayush Bhardwaj, Allos AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yphdry8ttAQ)**
Ayush Bhardwaj identifies a recurring failure mode in vertical AI — the inability to evaluate agent output in domains where the team lacks the practitioner judgment to recognize quality. He found this wall identical moving from a hedge fund to a pharma tech company.

- **Building the agent is the easy part; evaluating it is not.** Bhardwaj could construct the system. The problem was judgment: no one on his team had the instinct to recognize a weak trade thesis or a poor drug candidate the way an engineer instantly spots weak code.
- **The gap is domain fluency, not engineering capability.** An engineer's ability to evaluate generated code comes from years of exposure. That equivalent judgment doesn't exist on a team for specialized domains unless it's explicitly built in.
- **The parallel holds across industries.** Moving from a hedge fund to pharma, Bhardwaj expected a different problem and found the same wall — the domain changes, but the challenge of evaluating agent output without practitioner instinct is identical.

**[Healthcare's Agent Bytecode: X12 as the Harness for AI Agents — Vasant Kearney, Onlay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UyyOoJmuATU)**
Vasant Kearney argues that no standard payer data surface — phone system, web portal, or X12 feed — is ground truth, and they can contradict each other or agree on the wrong answer, making X12 the most reliable harness for AI agents navigating healthcare claims.

- **Three data surfaces can agree and still be wrong.** Phone, portal, and X12 may all confirm a patient is covered; the claim can still come back denied. Consistency across surfaces is not a validity signal.
- **Payer systems are often built by different teams, sometimes by different contractors.** This explains why contradiction is structural rather than exceptional — there is no single authoritative record, only multiple systems that may or may not be in sync.
- **X12 is the closest thing to an authoritative interface for agents.** Kearney positions X12 — the standard transaction format for healthcare claims — as the most reliable harness for AI agents that need to operate inside the claims workflow.

**[200 Million Patient Interactions Later — Vivek Muppalla, Hippocratic AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AN65uc645mE)**
Vivek Muppalla uses the almost-empty show of hands for "ever received a proactive call from your provider" to illustrate system-level scarcity — and Hippocratic AI's 200 million clinical conversations across 60+ health systems as evidence that the arithmetic can change.

- **Proactive outreach is the scarcity signal.** The system triages: only the sickest get a call. Almost no one in the room had ever received one. That gap is the problem Hippocratic AI targets.
- **200 million conversations across more than 60 health systems is the current scale.** This is the number Muppalla uses to mark the transition from interesting research to operational infrastructure.
- **The latency tradeoff was refused.** Models accurate enough for clinical work can take tens of seconds to respond. The talk frames how Hippocratic AI navigated that constraint to make real-time clinical conversation viable.

**[AI is the World's largest Relationship Therapist — Clay Cockrell & Tony Fabrikant, CoupleWork AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yoONZwV2smc)**
Clay Cockrell, a licensed therapist, frames the scale gap between organized mental health services and AI chat with two numbers — BetterHelp's roughly 35,000 clinicians serving 5 million users versus ChatGPT's roughly 900 million weekly active users, enough of whom open it after a fight to constitute de facto relationship counseling at population scale.

- **BetterHelp took a decade to assemble roughly 35,000 clinicians and 5 million users.** ChatGPT sees roughly 900 million weekly active users. The mismatch in scale defines the problem space.
- **Enough people open ChatGPT at 11pm after a fight that Cockrell names it the world's largest relationship therapist.** This is the observable behavior that motivates CoupleWork AI — not a theoretical concern but an existing pattern.
- **A licensed therapist is building the product because the field has structured knowledge about what works.** Cockrell's kept-broken loveseat — gravity tips couples toward each other however hard they grip the arms — is intentional. The same intentionality, he argues, should be built into AI-assisted relationship tools.

**[Shipping AI to a Million Patients Without an A/B Test — Jared Joselowitz, Ufonia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=McknwOzbmyg)**
Jared Joselowitz explains the evaluation constraints in clinical AI deployment — you cannot A/B test patients, cannot undo a spoken call, and a vendor benchmark is not a legal defense — and how Ufonia built an evaluation stack for Dora, a voice agent that has made roughly 200,000 clinical calls across 20 UK hospitals.

- **Clinical AI cannot use the standard evaluation playbook.** A/B testing means randomizing someone into the worse variant — unethical and often illegal. The voice call cannot be recalled once spoken. Each of these is a constraint with no engineering workaround.
- **A vendor's benchmark number is not a defense at a post-incident review.** This is Joselowitz's framing for why external benchmarks are insufficient: accountability in clinical settings requires evidence specific to your system's actual performance.
- **Dora has made roughly 200,000 real clinical calls across 20 UK hospitals and is contracted to reach a million patients.** The evaluation stack was built to make deployment at that scale defensible, not just technically functional.

**[Guardrails First: Engineering Member-Facing Health AI — Rashi Agrawal, Hinge Health](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YXEqC05WEI0)**
Rashi Agrawal opens with a concrete harm — a patient hospitalized for three weeks after an AI recommended sodium bromide as a salt substitute — and uses it alongside a Mount Sinai safety study and an ECRI hazard ranking to frame why guardrails must come first in member-facing health AI.

- **The harm case is concrete and documented.** A healthy 60-year-old man followed an AI dietary recommendation, arrived in an emergency room with bromide at 200 times the safe level, and stayed three weeks. The failure mode is not hypothetical.
- **Independent safety testing found undertriage of life-threatening emergencies roughly half the time.** The Mount Sinai study is Agrawal's evidence that the problem is systematic, not an isolated case.
- **ECRI named chatbot misuse the top health technology hazard.** This is the industry safety body's assessment — a signal that the field has identified the risk and is watching it.

**[From Ambient Documentation to Clinical Intelligence — Chaitanya Asawa, Abridge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u6q-byPWUuo)**
Chaitanya Asawa explains how Abridge started with "pajama time" — the roughly two hours per day clinicians spend on post-visit documentation — scaled to 300 of the largest US health systems in two to three years, and is repositioning that documentation layer as the entry point for broader clinical intelligence.

- **"Pajama time" is the problem statement.** Clinicians spend roughly two hours a day writing visit notes after the day ends. That is the friction Abridge targeted at the start.
- **The documentation product reached 300 of the largest health systems in two to three years.** The speed of adoption is the evidence that the problem was real and the solution fit.
- **Everything in healthcare sits downstream of one conversation.** Asawa's framing is that the doctor-patient conversation is the root data source, and administrative machinery — notes, orders, billing — was built around it rather than out of it. The ambient documentation layer is positioned as the entry point for turning that conversation into clinical intelligence.
