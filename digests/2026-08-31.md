## AI Engineer

**[SOTA Generative Media Panel — Dumitru Erhan, Shane Gu & Nicole Brichtova, Google DeepMind](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KLDdXOw6jIc)**
Google DeepMind researchers challenge human preference as a reliable evaluation signal for generative media, arguing it systematically rewards cosmetic artifacts over genuine realism.

- **Winning the eval is not winning the argument.** The team's model won human preference tests against real video — not because it was more realistic, but because its output was sharper, more saturated, and had more flattering skin tones.
- **The "Instagram filter" problem has a name now.** Nicole Brichtova's framing: tuning against human preference optimizes for the appeal of a filter, not fidelity to ground truth. The same blind spot appears across their image model work.
- **Human preference is an unreliable optimization target.** Erhan explicitly deflates his own team's favorable result — a signal that the research posture is honest about what the metric is and isn't measuring.
- **The implication for benchmarks is broad.** If a cosmetic bias can invert a preference eval on video, the same effect can distort any domain where "looks better" and "is better" diverge.

**[Tell the Robot What You Want — Sandhya Subramani, AWS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S6aSoQ6_u5A)**
Adding a language-model agent layer on top of an existing robot's motion policies enables open-ended natural-language queries without retraining the underlying movement stack — and exposes the gap between a working prototype and production robustness.

- **The architecture is layered, not replaced.** Scout's existing movement policies stay intact; the LLM agent sits above them, translating natural-language intent into calls the robot already understands.
- **Zero-shot generalization in hardware.** The rover answers "how many people can you see?" without ever being explicitly trained on that question, using its front camera at inference time and reporting two people.
- **The interface shift changes who can task a robot.** Moving from programmed commands to natural-language queries expands the operator pool beyond engineers — which is arguably the bigger capability gain.
- **Live demos still fall.** The demonstration doesn't go perfectly, which is an honest reminder that the gap between a Raspberry Pi rover on a SIM card and a production deployment remains substantial.

**[The Signal Layer: What to Build When Anything Can Be Built — Lena Hall, Akamai](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1KOdiGgMtpY)**
When AI makes every competent task trivially replicable at the same moment for every competitor, the source of advantage shifts entirely to proprietary signals — data and context that no model trained on common knowledge can reproduce.

- **The floor of effort just dropped to zero, symmetrically.** The same capability uplift reached every team on the same morning, which means average-quality work is no longer differentiation — it's table stakes.
- **Models answer from common knowledge.** Ask a model what to build or what users want and it returns the same confident, competent answer it gives everyone else. Competitive signal cannot come from that layer.
- **Operational leverage is already real, and already symmetric.** Running 18 agents from a bike is a genuine capability; the asymmetry comes only from what those agents are pointed at, not from having them.
- **The signal layer is proprietary context.** What competitors cannot replicate is what only your data can see: behavioral patterns, usage signals, and edge cases specific to your user base.

**[Tribal Dungeons of Global Shipping: AI Agents at Global Scale — Dmitry Buykin, Maersk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQ-_i1tZiws)**
Maersk's experience deploying agents at global scale shows that the primary obstacle is not model capability but the translation of human-legible procedures into machine-executable ones — a translation that turned out to be most of the project.

- **SOPs as screenshots are a dead end for agents.** Maersk's institutional knowledge lived in sequences of UI screenshots — sufficient for onboarding a person, completely opaque to anything that needs to execute against a backend.
- **"Tribal dungeons" names the real problem.** The knowledge existed; it just wasn't encoded in a form anything could execute safely without a human interpreting context mid-step.
- **Agents require far more structure than humans do.** Each procedure needed explicit preconditions, branch decisions, identifiers, backend call specs, validation rules, recovery paths, and audit evidence — none of which was embedded in the original SOP.
- **The hard work was translation, not training.** The majority of the project was converting existing procedures into agent-executable form, not building or tuning a model.

**[Agentic Sites: Building Hyper Personalized Websites — Carlos Sanchez, Adobe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jebp4V0vh30)**
Adobe is demonstrating that fully generated, per-query web pages — assembled from a single natural-language request in under two seconds — represent a practical path toward the "audience of one" ideal that marketers have pursued for decades without an affordable cost model.

- **A page, not a search result.** The output is a complete page: product selection scoped to the query, rewritten copy, and contextual tips — generated specifically for one request, not a ranked list of generic links.
- **Sub-two-second generation is the bar.** The demo assembles a full personalized page in under two seconds, making it viable as a real-time experience rather than a batch-rendered asset.
- **"Audience of one" finally has a cost model.** Per-user personalization has been structurally unaffordable; generated pages make the marginal cost of a new page near zero.
- **The tool generalizes to any URL.** Adobe's system can build a comparable generated experience for any provided URL in roughly an hour, lowering the adoption bar for teams outside Adobe's own stack.

**[Agents Are Where Microservices Were in 2015 — Roberto Milev & Uday Kanagala, Navan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=32nrHU6zHU8)**
Navan's experience building travel and expense agents surfaces a foundational identity and authorization problem: the existing model of "user or service principal" does not survive contact with agents that act asynchronously on deferred intent.

- **Deferred actions break identity.** When an agent fires a purchase two weeks after a human instruction, existing audit trails and authorization models cannot cleanly attribute or validate the action.
- **"User or service account" is a false binary.** Agents operate in a hybrid mode — acting on behalf of a user but potentially under a service identity — and current permission systems were not designed for that overlap.
- **Authorization in travel means financial authorization.** Navan's domain makes the stakes concrete: a mis-attributed agent action is not just a logging problem, it is a purchase someone may not have consented to.
- **The 2015 microservices parallel is structural.** As with microservices, the tooling for agents — auth, observability, governance — is still catching up to the architectural pattern already being deployed in production.

**[AI Agents Are Just Distributed Systems Now — Salman Munaf, TikTok](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hD9-V56FNRI)**
TikTok's site reliability perspective reframes AI agents as distributed systems, arguing that every reliability pattern from SRE — idempotency, retry logic, status lookups — applies directly and non-optionally to agent tool calls.

- **Timeout means unknown, not failure.** An agent's default instinct on any error is to retry; without idempotency keys, a timed-out refund call becomes a double refund.
- **Request identifiers are not optional.** Deduplicating retries requires a stable ID that survives the original call, the timeout, and any re-attempt — the same requirement distributed systems have enforced for years.
- **The moment a model calls an external service, SRE rules apply.** Crossing the API boundary transforms an LLM into a distributed system with all the failure modes that entails.
- **Reliability is not a layer you add later.** Teams shipping agents without status-lookup endpoints and idempotent tool interfaces are accumulating distributed-systems debt — the double-refund scenario is the canonical failure mode.

**[From Tokenmaxxing to Trusted Throughput — Mingsheng Hong, Ironclad](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dSg0pu8d6qg)**
Ironclad's approach to AI governance treats token consumption as a diagnostic signal worth monitoring for anomalies in both directions — rejecting the common failure mode of rewarding high usage as a proxy for AI adoption.

- **Token dashboards as smoke detectors, not scoreboards.** High usage triggers review; so does suspiciously low usage. A team consuming very few tokens is worth a conversation, not a gold star.
- **The lines-of-code analogy holds.** Optimizing for token count — up or down — is as counterproductive as optimizing for lines of code written; what matters is the outcome the tokens enabled.
- **Rewarding consumption creates perverse incentives.** Colleagues competing to top a usage leaderboard is a concrete failure mode Hong's team explicitly avoided by treating the dashboard as diagnostic rather than evaluative.
- **Setting the norm early is the governance move.** Establishing the "smoke detector" frame before teams form habits around usage metrics avoids the harder cultural reset required once a leaderboard dynamic is entrenched.

**[Guardians of the State: An Air-Gapped AI Fortress for Consumer Data — Rachna Srivastava, DFPI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2WZsT-znFTQ)**
California's Department of Financial Protection and Innovation chose physical data-flow constraints over configurable software controls for its consumer financial data AI system, treating misconfiguration itself as an unacceptable attack surface.

- **Physics over policy.** The air gap is implemented by severing the fiber optic cable: data can physically enter the building but cannot leave, because there is no outbound transmitter — eliminating the entire exfiltration risk class that software firewalls only mitigate.
- **Misconfiguration is the real threat model.** Srivastava's team chose physical controls specifically because any software firewall can be misconfigured, and a misconfigured secure system is functionally an exploited one.
- **The constraint shapes every design decision.** Everything the group builds must function within the air-gap architecture — models, pipelines, and interfaces all have to work without outbound connectivity.
- **State-level AI deployments have a different threat surface.** Handling consumer financial data under a regulatory mandate creates pressure to eliminate risk classes rather than manage them; the air-gap reflects the conclusion that a misconfiguration-proof control is worth any operational constraint it introduces.
