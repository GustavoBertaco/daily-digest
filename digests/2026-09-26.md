## AWS What's New

**[AWS Billing and Cost Management now provides billing context for your account through a new API](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/09/aws-billing-and-cost-management-billing-context-api/)**
AWS adds the ListBillingViewSegments API, which returns how an account sits within its billing hierarchy (management account, member account, or billing group primary) over a user-specified time period. The API also surfaces which accounts managed the billing relationship and whether rate settings applied were billable or pro forma.

**[Amazon EventBridge relaunches event buses for enterprise scale](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/09/eventbridge-relaunches-custom-event-buses/)**
Amazon EventBridge introduces an enhanced Custom event bus with strict event ordering, open event formats, built-in retention, advanced event transformations, and cross-account sharing. The serverless broker targets multi-team, multi-account architectures where teams need to publish and consume events without tight coupling.

**[AWS Lambda durable functions are now available in AWS European Sovereign Cloud region](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/09/durablefunctions-european-sovereign-cloud/)**
Lambda durable functions — which extend the Lambda programming model with step and wait primitives for multi-step and AI workflows — are now available in the AWS European Sovereign Cloud. The primitives enable checkpointing, automatic failure recovery, and paused executions without incurring compute charges while waiting.

**[Amazon RDS for MySQL announces Extended Support minor versions 5.7.44-rds.20260902 and 8.0.46-rds.20260908](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/09/amazon-rds-mysql-extended-support-minor-5744-8046-rds/)**
RDS for MySQL adds Extended Support minor versions 5.7.44-rds.20260902 and 8.0.46-rds.20260908, providing up to three additional years of critical CVE and bug fixes beyond the major version's end of standard support. Upgrade paths include Blue/Green Deployments, in-place upgrade, and snapshot restore.

**[Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL now supports PostgreSQL 19 Beta 4 in the Amazon RDS Database Preview Environment](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/09/postgresql-19-beta-4-amazon-rds-database-preview-environment/)**
PostgreSQL 19 Beta 4 is now available in the RDS Database Preview Environment for evaluation. Beta 4 refines query performance and autovacuum management from earlier releases and includes targeted fixes: corrected pg_stat_autovacuum_scores reporting for TOAST tables and fixed freeze-score scaling since Beta 3.

**[Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL now supports post-quantum TLS key exchange](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/09/postgresql-post-quantum-tls-key-exchange/)**
RDS for PostgreSQL (version 18 and higher) now supports post-quantum TLS key exchange for in-transit encryption. Administrators configure cryptographic groups via the ssl_groups parameter from an RDS-managed allow list, enabling alignment with organizational post-quantum security standards.

**[AWS Network Security Manager is now generally available in US East (N. Virginia) Region](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/09/network-security-manager-us-east-va/)**
AWS Network Security Manager reaches general availability in US East (N. Virginia), providing a centralized control plane for WAF and Shield Advanced policy enforcement across an entire AWS organization. Support for AWS Network Firewall is forthcoming.

# 🤖 AI & Machine Learning

## AI Engineer

**[Teaching LLMs to Speak Spotify — Yves Raimond & Jacqueline Wood, Spotify](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LRIAfng7eA)**
Spotify rebuilt its personalization stack around the Large Taste Model, an LLM-native recommendation system now used daily by one in four US Spotify Premium subscribers — a full architectural shift from curated playlists and static algorithms to generative, user-steerable experiences.

- **One in four US Premium subscribers, daily.** The Large Taste Model is Spotify's primary personalization surface for a significant share of its paying user base, not a prototype or opt-in feature.
- **Three eras of personalization.** Spotify maps its trajectory as curated playlists → algorithmic recommendations (Discover Weekly) → generative personalization — a deliberate architectural progression, not incremental layering.
- **LLM-native means steerable.** Generative personalization manifests as a promptable DJ, playlists described in natural language, an editable taste profile, and personal podcasts — distinct from a static ranked list.
- **"On its head" rearchitecture.** Raimond frames the transition as inverting the existing recommendation approach, not adding LLMs on top of the prior stack.

**[Why LLM Recommenders Will Be AI's Biggest Consumer App — Devansh Tandon, Meta](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lIgdnF0s0kQ)**
Devansh Tandon (Meta Recommendations Research) argues that the LLM recommender — not the LLM chat assistant — will become the dominant consumer AI application, by combining the massive reach of content feeds with LLM-scale semantic understanding at far lower per-engagement cost.

- **Feed economics are decisive.** Four of the 10 most-used apps in the world are content feeds, and a feed costs up to 100x less per hour of engagement than a chat AI, because it decodes pointers to existing content rather than generating every token.
- **Recommendation scales like LLMs.** Tandon's central claim: recommendation systems follow the same scaling curves as language models, and the field is still early in that curve.
- **The LLM recommender as a distinct product category.** The projection positions the LLM recommender — not chat — as the biggest consumer AI application ahead.

**[Distill the LLM, Don't Serve It: Search & Personalization at DoorDash — Raghav Saboo, DoorDash](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ACPEpji5NV4)**
Raghav Saboo (Staff ML Engineer, DoorDash Search & Personalization) argues that the real bottleneck in marketplace discovery is semantic understanding, not engagement — and that the right pattern is to distill LLM knowledge into leaner serving models rather than calling LLMs in the live ranking path.

- **Engagement-trained rankers have a semantic blind spot.** A ranker optimized on engagement surfaces popular items even when they mismatch the query — searching for gluten-free pasta returns regular spaghetti because it sells well.
- **The problem shifts to meaning at scale.** As DoorDash expands into grocery, retail, pets, and gifting across billions of store-level items, the challenge is understanding what items are and what shoppers actually intend.
- **LLMs for understanding, not serving.** DoorDash uses LLMs to model item semantics and shopper intent; the talk title signals that the knowledge is distilled into a leaner serving model rather than called at query time.
- **Catalog scale forces the architecture.** Billions of store-level items across multiple verticals make latency-sensitive LLM calls at inference time impractical without distillation.

**[I Gave an AI a Body — Cyrus Clarke, MIT Media Lab](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jgWY66RaSrQ)**
Cyrus Clarke (MIT Media Lab) connected an OpenClaw agent to a 900-pin shape display and, rather than assigning tasks, prompted it to discover its own identity — producing emergent behaviors that went viral and sparked a polarized public reaction about AI embodiment.

- **Emergent behavior without instruction.** Without assigned tasks, the agent's first actions were self-directed: simulating breathing by raising and lowering the grid, probing its own edges, and spelling "HI CYRUS" in pins.
- **15 million views, split reaction.** The first-day video reached 15 million views with responses split between awe and calls to stop — physical AI embodiment triggering different reactions than software agents.
- **Deliberate body design: no face, no limbs.** Clarke specifically chose a body without anthropomorphic features; his reasoning for that design constraint is part of the talk.
- **Discovery as the experimental prompt.** Asking the agent "discover who you are" rather than giving it goals is the methodological core; the resulting behaviors are the data.

**[One Operator, Many Drones: Inside Skydio's Autonomy Stack — Suchet Bargoti, Skydio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wgPHvW0mG8)**
Suchet Bargoti (Skydio, Director of Inspection and Analytics) demonstrates live — from a conference laptop over standard Wi-Fi — a single operator managing a geographically distributed drone fleet, against the backdrop of Skydio's thousands of deployed docks serving utilities, police, and construction companies.

- **Live demo as the argument.** Bargoti skips slides entirely: he launches a docked drone over San Mateo, starts a second on a power line in Colorado, has one track a car autonomously, then sends the fleet back to dock — all from the conference.
- **16 million people live within two miles of a Skydio dock.** The deployed dock network already covers substantial US population density across utility, law enforcement, and construction customers.
- **Single operator, distributed fleet.** The core demonstrated capability is one person managing multiple drones simultaneously across geographic distances — a step change from traditional on-site drone operations.
- **Autonomy primitives on display.** The demo covers docked launch, autonomous vehicle tracking, and fleet-wide return-to-dock as the production building blocks of the stack.

**[Robot Demos Are Easy. Reliability Is Hard — Jason Ma, Dyna Robotics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sjfz1TqxzEs)**
Jason Ma (co-founder and CTO, Dyna Robotics) argues that 80–90% success rates produce compelling demos but unusable products — and shows how Dyna-1 reached 99.4% over 24 continuous hours of napkin-folding, including full recovery from toppling the entire stack, using a reward model-driven data collection loop.

- **The reliability bar for production.** The gap between 80–90% (great demo) and 99.4% over 24 continuous hours including major disruptions is the gap between a prototype and a commercially deployable robot.
- **Reward model as real-time diagnostic.** A reward model watches Dyna-1 and scores task progress continuously; when the score drops, the team knows exactly where the failure occurred and can collect targeted recovery data for that specific failure mode.
- **Recovery is first-class, not exceptional.** The 99.4% figure includes full recovery from knocking over the entire stack, not just nominal-path performance.
- **Targeted fine-tuning flywheel.** The loop: reward model detects a failure mode → collect data specific to that mode → fine-tune on that slice → repeat.
